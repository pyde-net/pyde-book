# Why Pyde?

The global economy is deeply digital, but it is not digitally unified.

Banks, sovereign monetary systems, enterprises, registries, exchanges, payment networks, and public applications each represent part of economic life. Existing financial and distributed systems already solve important pieces of the problem. The gap is how independently governed economic systems coordinate when their state, authority, and rules differ.

That problem becomes more visible across borders.

A cross border economic relationship may require identity, authorization, ownership information, foreign exchange, liquidity, settlement, compliance, and reconciliation to line up across multiple systems. The payment itself is only one state transition inside a larger economic relationship.

Pyde approaches the problem as distributed ledger infrastructure rather than as another isolated asset network.

The key abstraction is **global economic state**. A balance, ownership relationship, authorization, regulatory status, obligation, settlement position, or contract state can be represented as programmable state. Distributed ledger technology provides the shared and verifiable foundation for those state transitions where the relevant participants authorize shared coordination.

## Why three environments?

Different economic systems require different authority models.

A sovereign monetary system cannot operate like a permissionless application.

An open settlement network cannot become the owner of the sovereign currencies it coordinates.

A public application cannot require every participant to be a licensed institution.

Pyde therefore separates the architecture into three environments.

| Tier | Role |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Sovereign economic networks |
| Tier 2 | Open interlinking settlement |
| Tier 3 | Permissionless public infrastructure |

The same distributed ledger foundation can support all three without pretending their authority models are identical.

## The objective

Pyde is not trying to replace every existing payment network, bank, database, ledger, or public blockchain.

The objective is to provide common distributed ledger infrastructure through which independent economic systems can represent, verify, and coordinate state while retaining the authority associated with their own domains.

That is the Pyde thesis in practical form.
